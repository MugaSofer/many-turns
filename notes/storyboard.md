Timings from audio/take4-timing.json. One beat ≈ 0.58s; one 4/4 bar ≈ 2.3s.

## Recurring motifs
- **The turn.** The world rotates between scenes (the vase turns, or the page turns). "Many turns", made literal.
- **The oar.** Always visible on or near him: a stick, then a weight, then plainly an oar. Two oars get planted:
  Elpenor's on a grave by the sea (2:48), his own inland (5:15). The last shot shows both.
- **Words as matter.** In the underworld, ghosts are made of handwriting. The tree's roots are lines of writing.
  This is the only place the AI layer shows visually, and even there it reads as poetic, not techy.
- **The watchers.** Faces lit by firelight, listening. Every hall is a different audience.

## If the vase style wins: vary the vessel with the story (proposal, 2026-09-27)
Greek potters used three techniques, and they fit the song's sections:
- **Black-figure** (black glaze figures on orange clay): the voyage, the halls, the gods. As in the tests.
- **Red-figure** (the inverse: clay-coloured figures on black): night and homecoming (Ithaca, Penelope, the tree's roots).
  It also echoes the "Nobody" moment, where he becomes an outline.
- **White-ground lekythos** (fine line drawing on white slip): these were *funeral* vases, painted for the dead. The
  underworld and Elpenor, with ghosts drawn in faint line and handwriting.
Transitions: the camera pulls back from one vase, and we see it standing among others on a dark shelf, then pushes into the
next. The final shot could pull back to show all three vases together, with the oar-tree painted across them.
