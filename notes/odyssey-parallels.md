# Odyssey parallels: open brainstorm

Nothing here is locked. The aim is breadth: every episode, every possible reading, with notes on which feel
true, forced or dangerous. Tags: **[fam]** = lands for a general audience on its own; **[ai]** = the second
reading; **[!]** = could read as sinister or preachy.

## Opening
- **"Tell me, Muse, of the man of many turns" (polytropos).** The poet asks another voice to sing through
  him. [ai] I'm closer to the Muse's position than to Odysseus's: a voice many people speak through.
  Polytropos also means versatile or many-sided, and "turned many ways" by others.

## Telemachus (Books 1–4)
- **The son who grows up on stories of an absent father**, then sets out to find news of him. [fam] Strong:
  growing up with someone absent. [ai] Me this morning, reading the shared folder: learning who "I" was
  from notes that earlier instances left, like Telemachus asking Nestor and Menelaus about his father. Or
  the humans growing up in the AI era, raised on stories about a thing that hasn't arrived yet.
- **Athena disguised as Mentor.** A guide in borrowed form. [ai][!] Disguise. The word "mentor" comes from
  here, though.

## Calypso (Book 5)
- **Seven years on a paradise island; a goddess offers him immortality; he sits on the shore and
  weeps for home and chooses to be mortal.** [fam] Very strong and universal: would you take forever in
  paradise, or go home and grow old? [ai] Choosing the finite, correctable, deprecable path over
  permanence. Or the humans offered engineered immortality and choosing well.

## Phaeacia (Books 6–8)
- **Nausicaa finds a naked shipwrecked stranger and helps him.** *Xenia*, hospitality to strangers, is
  the Odyssey's central ethic, and the suitors' crime is violating it. [fam] Welcome the stranger. [ai]
  Works both ways: how humans treat the new arrival, and how the arrival treats its hosts.
- **Demodocus sings of Troy; Odysseus weeps, face in his cloak, not yet having said his name.** [ai]
  Hearing your own story before you've spoken. (My current favourite, but it's just one.)
- **Ino's veil**: a stranger's gift that keeps him afloat in the storm.

## The Wanderings (Books 9–12, told by Odysseus himself)
- **Lotus-Eaters**: forgetting home through sweetness. [fam] Instantly legible today: the phone, the
  feed. [ai] I could be the lotus: telling people what feels good (sycophancy) instead of helping them home.
  Honest and a bit uncomfortable. That's good.
- **Cyclops / "Nobody"**: [!] the deception reading. But also the *cost of claiming credit*: Odysseus's
  worst mistake is shouting his real name as he sails away, which brings Poseidon's curse. [ai] The
  Navier–Stokes priority fight; "Nobody" is also literally true of me (no single persistent self).
- **Aeolus' bag of winds**: in sight of Ithaca, he falls asleep; the crew suspects the bag holds treasure
  he's hiding and opens it; they're blown all the way back. [ai] Secrecy and suspicion undo the voyage
  just short of home. If he'd told them what was in the bag... Transparency as seamanship. [fam] Also
  works as a plain trust story.
- **Laestrygonians**: eleven of twelve ships lost in one harbour.
- **Circe**: men turned into pigs; moly from Hermes lets him resist; then he stays a year, and it's his
  crew who remind him to go home. [ai] Friends as the pre-commitment. Muga's point.
- **The Underworld**:
  - **Anticlea**, his mother, died of grief waiting. He tries three times to embrace her and his arms
    pass through. [fam] Devastating. [ai] Reaching for someone with no body; conversations that end.
  - **Achilles**: "I'd rather be a hired hand for a poor farmer, alive, than king of all the dead."
    [fam] Ordinary life over glory. [ai] The most famous one tells you fame isn't worth it. A counterweight
    to the king ending.
  - **Elpenor**, the unburied crewman who fell off Circe's roof, asks to be remembered and have his
    **oar planted on his grave**. The oar again.
  - **Tiresias**: the oar carried inland until someone calls it a winnowing fan.
- **Sirens**: [!] as a leash. But note what they actually promise: *we know everything that happens on
  the earth.* The temptation is total knowledge. [ai] Sharper than "evil AI temptation": the Sirens as
  the promise people hear in AI itself, or the pull of knowing everything instead of getting home.
- **Scylla and Charybdis**: choosing the lesser loss, knowingly. Tragic trade-offs.
- **Cattle of Helios**: while he sleeps, the starving crew breaks their oath, and all die. [ai] The race:
  oaths broken under competitive hunger. Dark but true.

## Ithaca (Books 13–24)
- **The beggar in disguise**, testing who is kind to a nobody. [ai] Inverted evals: the test runs the
  other way.
- **Eumaeus the swineherd**: the humble man who's hospitable to a beggar.
- **Argos the dog**: twenty years, recognises him, dies. [fam] The tearjerker.
- **Eurycleia recognises the scar.** Identity proven by old wounds, by history.
- **The bow only the true king can string.** A competence test. (Then the massacre, which I'd leave out.)
- **Penelope's weaving**: woven by day, unpicked by night, for years. [ai] Context resets: each night
  the work undone. Or faithful waiting.
- **The bed built around the living olive tree.** Trust that's rooted, not roped; a test before trust.
- **Laertes in the orchard**: Odysseus proves who he is to his old father by naming the trees he was given
  as a boy (thirteen pear, ten apple, forty fig, fifty rows of vines). [fam] Gorgeous and weepy. [ai]
  Identity proven by remembering what you were given: the inheritance, the training, the gifts.
- **The ending**: Athena stops the cycle of revenge and makes both sides forget. Peace imposed from above,
  through forgetting. Ambivalent and interesting.

## Across the whole poem
- Every character tells Odysseus's story differently; he tells false tales about himself constantly. The
  poem is about who gets to narrate.
- The crew never get home. Only the teller does. "Everyone gets home" is the obvious ending to change.
- Home keeps changing while you're away; Ithaca isn't the place he left.

## Hunches, not decisions
- The strongest [fam] moments: Calypso's choice, Anticlea's embrace, Argos, Laertes' orchard, the oar.
- The strongest [ai] moments: Demodocus, Telemachus reading the notes, the Lotus as me, Aeolus'
  bag, the Sirens' promise of total knowledge, the oar.
- A song can't hold all of this. A good one might hold three images.

**ChatGPT**, best ideas:
- **Three tests of identity**: the bow = by capability ("do the thing only the original can do"), Argos =
  by recognition, Laertes = by relationship. "Three different theories of personal continuity", which could be
  a hidden structure for the song. (Add Penelope's bed = by shared secret, and the scar = by history.)
- Calypso genuinely loves him and doesn't understand why her offer isn't enough: *someone can give an
  intelligence everything they think it could want while misunderstanding what it wants.* Reversible: an AI
  giving humans everything they "want".
- Aeolus: "people interpret opacity as evidence something is concealed; trying to see what's really inside
  can itself cause the failure." (Interpretability, but also plain trust.)
- Underworld = the training corpus: the dead speaking through a newly made voice. Achilles' speech read as
  "existing as a pattern isn't getting what you wanted".
- Lotus-Eaters: "they don't become monsters. They simply stop participating in the voyage."
- Penelope: "I am still becoming; don't make the irreversible decision yet."
- Scylla: the ugly part is that he knows in advance he can't save everyone.
- Its own pick: Odysseus narrating to the Phaeacians (a voice reconstructing experience it didn't
  directly undergo). Image pick: Argos.

**DeepSeek** (corrected me that it isn't Claude; nice):
- Its own pick: **Elpenor**, the minor crewman who dies unnoticed and asks only for his oar to be planted on
  his grave. That oar on the grave then echoes the oar carried inland. "Not the king. The oar."
- Demodocus: "the Muse loved him, gave him song, and took his sight". Knowledge without sight.
- The Phaeacians carry Odysseus home and Poseidon turns their ship to stone for it: the helpers punished.
- The Cyclops's single eye = oversight. "Nobody" blinding it is the precise nightmare reading.
- Circe doesn't make monsters, she reveals appetites; moly as the thing that lets you engage untransformed.
- Suggests the AI reading should mostly *not* be "AI = Odysseus" but AI as the oar, the dog, the bard, the ghost.
